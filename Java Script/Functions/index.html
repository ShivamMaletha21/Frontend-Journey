Why "Creative Brand Positioning Studio" Actually Works
The name gap is real — and you spotted it
  • Branding is commoditized — every freelancer says "I do branding." Positioning is a strategy word — it signals you think, not just design. This alone separates you.
  • D2C brands are positioning-obsessed — they know their product isn't unique. They need to FEEL unique. That's your exact service.
  • India has zero globally known brand studios — there's no Indian Pentagram or Wolff Olins. The space is wide open for a studio that builds a brand around its own brand.
  • AI as execution speed — a D2C brand spending ₹3L/month at an agency gets 20 posts. You give them 40 posts + strategy + positioning doc for ₹1.5L. That's your price vs value story.
  • The small team is a feature, not a bug — "We're a lean, focused studio" is a positioning statement. One or two creative minds obsessed with your brand > 10 distracted agency employees.
The Hard Truths — Don't Ignore These
  • Instagram alone won't get you clients for months — it builds credibility, not pipeline. You need direct outreach now, Instagram later.
  • No portfolio = no trust — your first 2–3 projects must exist, even if you create them for free or for yourself as case studies.
  • Subscriptions are a real problem — Midjourney, Adobe, Runway all cost money. Solve this with free tiers + one shared subscription until revenue hits.
  • Kokoro needs to look like a studio, not two friends with an Instagram — your visual identity must be extraordinary before you pitch anyone.
  • D2C brands have slow procurement — budget approvals, legal, NDAs take 4–6 weeks even when they love you. Plan your cash flow accordingly.

Cold Outreach Script That Works
The 3-line formula
  • Line 1 — Specific observation: "I noticed your packaging copy says premium but your Instagram grid feels entry-level."
  • Line 2 — What you do: "Kokoro is a brand positioning studio — we close that gap for D2C brands."

Pricing Philosophy — Never Undersell
  • Price is a positioning signal — a D2C brand paying ₹8,000 for 20 posts thinks you're a freelancer. ₹40,000 makes you a studio.
  • Split all revenue 50/50 — you and your partner take equal share from the start. This prevents resentment and builds real partnership.
  • Invoice immediately — 50% advance, 50% on delivery. Never 100% on delivery for new clients.
  • No discounts — add value instead — if they want a lower price, don't discount. Reduce scope or add a bonus deliverable at the same price.

The Freelancer Network Model
Your vision of contracting freelancers instead of hiring is exactly right. Here's how to build it properly.
Why This Model Is Brilliant
You're building a creative collective, not a company

Kokoro stays small (you + partner) at the core. But you have a network of 8–12 trusted freelancers across disciplines that you can activate per project. No salaries. No HR. No office. Pure creative output with maximum flexibility. This is how studios like Pentagram, Sagmeister & Walsh work — small core, project-based expansion.

Roles to Build Into Your Network
  • Video editor — for Reels + ad videos (₹8,000–15,000/project)
  • Copywriter — brand voice, website copy (₹5,000–12,000/project)
  • Motion designer — animated posts, intro loops (₹10,000–20,000/project)
  • Photographer — product shoots (₹5,000–15,000/shoot)
  • Web designer — for brands that need a site refresh (₹15,000–40,000)
  • Strategist — for large rebrands (₹15,000–30,000/project)
Where to Find Good Freelancers
  • Behance — best for designers and motion artists
  • Dribbble — UI/visual designers
  • Instagram creative community — DM people whose work you love
  • LinkedIn — search "freelance video editor India"
  • Design colleges — NID, MIT Pune, Srishti grads looking for projects
  • Internshala / Unstop — for budget-conscious starter projects
How to Structure Freelancer Relationships
  • Test before you trust — give a small paid test (₹1,000–2,000) before onboarding anyone to your network. See their process, speed, and revision behavior.
  • Keep a rate card for each person — know exactly what each person charges before you commit to a client price. Your margin should be 40–60% on subcontracted work.
  • Simple contract for each project — 1-page agreement: deliverables, timeline, payment, IP ownership. No freelancer work should happen without this.
  • Position them as "Kokoro network collaborators" — don't call them employees or contractors in client conversations. "We work with a curated network of specialists" sounds premium.
  • Share credit selectively — some clients love knowing your collaborators. Others want to feel they're working with you only. Read the client.
